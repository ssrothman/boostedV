\section{Sensitivity of the E2C to the diboson and $ZH$ signals}
\label{app:sens}

This appendix collects the studies of how well the jet E2C, and correlators built from the stored particles, separate
the $WZ$ + $ZZ$ (``VV'') and $ZH(b\bar b)$ signals from the Drell--Yan background in the selection of this note. They
are statistical studies on simulation: no systematic uncertainties, no data. The working log with all numbers and
commands is \code{analysis/SENSITIVITY\_JOURNAL.md}; the plans are \code{analysis/SENSITIVITY\_PLAN.md} and
\code{analysis/E2C\_NEXT\_PLAN.md}, and the decisions taken along the way are in \code{analysis/E2C\_NEXT\_QUESTIONS.md}.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Method}
\label{app:sens:method}

\paragraph{Per-jet tables.} The analysis framework writes one row per selected jet (table key \code{tables},
\code{bvana/tables.py}): kinematics of the jet and the $Z$ candidate, substructure, tagger scores, MET, AK4 summaries and,
for each correlator, the jet's histogram as one row. The back-to-back selection (Section~\ref{sec:b2b}) keeps one jet
per event (checked on one Drell--Yan file: one jet in 14194 of 14194 events), so the per-jet covariance is the per-event
one. Weights are the expected yields at $\mathcal{L} = 326.03\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$, a full Run~3 placeholder.

\paragraph{Significance.} For a vector $v$ per jet (the concatenation of its correlator rows in slices of jet or $Z$
$\pt$), $H = \sum_\text{jets} w\,v$, the covariance at the luminosity is $C = \sum w\,v v^\mathsf{T}$ and the Monte Carlo
statistical covariance is $C_\mathrm{MC} = \sum w^2\,v v^\mathsf{T}$ (\code{bvana/sensitivity.py}). With signal $s$ and
background $b$, $Z_\mathrm{tot}^2 = s^\mathsf{T} C_b^{+} s$, and $Z_\mathrm{shape}$ is the same quantity with the
background normalisation profiled. The numbers quoted in this appendix are $Z_\mathrm{shape}$ with
$C_b + C_{\mathrm{MC},b} + C_{\mathrm{MC},s}$, restricted to bins with background MC. Without that restriction a bin
holding only signal MC gets the signal MC variance as its uncertainty and raises $Z$; this happened in the first version
and was fixed (a unit test fails without the restriction), and all scans were rerun.

\paragraph{Stability.} $s^\mathsf{T}\hat C^{+}s$ with an estimated covariance is biased upwards, by an amount that grows
with the number of bins per MC jet. Each working point therefore also gets $Z_\mathrm{shape}$ (without the MC term) from
two random halves of the MC with doubled weights. A point is called \emph{stable} if both half-sample values agree
within 10\%. A toy with 200 bins and a known covariance shows the size of the effect: 2000 MC jets give $Z = 294$ for a
true 277, 20000 give 278.8.

\paragraph{Background distortion.} A cut that sculpts the Drell--Yan shape makes the shape fit depend on the modelling of
that sculpting. Each cut is reported with the $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ of the Drell--Yan shape after the cut against the
baseline shape, each $\pt$ slice normalised separately, at the luminosity, and with the largest per-bin $|$ratio$-1|$
among bins with MC relative error below 5\%.

\paragraph{Baseline and signals.} Baseline: $\ptll > 200\GeV$, $\ptj > 200\GeV$, $|\eta^\mathrm{jet}| < 2.0$, medium
muon identification, both channels combined. VV and $ZH$ are optimised separately, each with the other in the
background. Tagger working points are set at fixed fractions of the baseline Drell--Yan yield (``DY efficiency''),
globally or per $\pt$ slice.

\paragraph{Signal jets that are not the boson.} Since 2026-11-20, signal jets not matched to the hadronic boson
(truth status $\neq 0$, Section~\ref{app:sens:truth}) are background (groups \code{VVother}, \code{ZHother}); before,
every jet of a signal sample was signal. Results below say which definition they use.

\paragraph{Tests.} The table code, the significance, the per-slice thresholds, the truth split and the condor tools have
unit tests, each shown to be discriminating by mutation (a deliberately broken line makes a test fail).

\subsection{Results on the existing productions}
\label{app:sens:phaseA}

Tables from the back-to-back productions of all processes (\code{20261116\_zxb2b}, \code{20261117\_zxb2bbkg},
\code{20261117\_zh}; Drell--Yan only with $\ptll > 200\GeV$); the jet E2C uses the charged constituents
(Section~\ref{sec:eec}). All jets of the signal samples are signal here.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Baseline.} VV: S/B 2.9\% in the boson-mass peak window, $Z_\mathrm{shape} = 9.2$ ($Z_\mathrm{tot} = 14.6$).
  $ZH$: S/B 0.14\%, $Z_\mathrm{shape} = 0.5$.
\item \textbf{Event-level cuts} ($\ptll$, $\ptj$, $|\eta|$, $\ptj/\ptll$, $m_{\ell\ell}$, MET, AK4 b-tag and
  multiplicity, $|y_Z|$, $|\Delta y|$, $|\cos\theta^*|$) raise the VV $Z_\mathrm{shape}$ of the $n = 1$ E2C by at most
  2\% (MET $< 100\GeV$: 9.4).
\item \textbf{Taggers.} The mass-decorrelated GloParT~3 V score
  $g_V = (X_{qq}+X_{cs}+X_{bb}+X_{cc})/(X_{qq}+X_{cs}+X_{bb}+X_{cc}+\mathrm{QCD})$ gives the only large gain:
  11.6 / 13.5 at 10\% / 3\% DY efficiency, with a residual Drell--Yan sculpting ($\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ 22 / 12, largest
  deviation 46\% / 61\%). Mass-correlated selections (soft-drop mass window, the \code{withMass} taggers, $\tau_{21}$,
  $N_2$, ParticleNet $X_{qq}$) sculpt strongly ($\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ $10^2$--$10^3$) and lower $Z_\mathrm{shape}$. Cuts
  on the jet's own E2C (peak-window fraction, a per-jet likelihood ratio) cut on the information the shape fit uses:
  $Z_\mathrm{shape} \le 8.2$, $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ $10^3$--$10^4$.
\item \textbf{Working points.} VV with $g_V$ is stable down to 1\% DY efficiency with $n = 1$ and 5 slices
  ($Z_\mathrm{shape} \approx 14$) and down to 3\% with 10 slices or both $n$ (14.4--15.3). $ZH$ with the ParticleNet
  $X_{bb}$-vs-QCD score reaches 1.2--2.4 at stable points, depending on the binning (Table~\ref{tab:sens:binning});
  larger values occur only at unstable ones. The GloParT bb
  score is worse than no cut for VV (it removes $W\to q\bar q'$); $g_V$ for $ZH$ reaches 0.8--1.9. Points at
  $\le 1\%$ DY efficiency have $\le 40$k Drell--Yan MC jets for 400--800 bins and cannot be judged with the available MC.
\item \textbf{Observable and binning} (best stable point, Table~\ref{tab:sens:binning}): $n = 1$ beats $n = 2$
  (baseline 9.2 vs 6.4; both together 9.5). Fewer $\Delta R$ bins lower $Z$. Binning in $\ptj\Delta R$ with one $\pt$
  slice equals $\Delta R$ with 5--10 slices, i.e.\ it replaces the slicing but adds nothing. $|\eta|$ slices bring no
  consistent gain. The stat-only soft-drop-mass template fit beats the E2C (VV 19--20, $ZH$ 4.9--5.5); adding the E2C
  to it changes VV by less than 1. The E2C was kept as the search observable by decision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Truth match, boson-$\pt$ scale and resolution}
\label{app:sens:truth}

\paragraph{Truth match.} The rules of the production truth axis (Section~\ref{sec:axes}) applied offline to the stored
generator record (\code{bvana/truth.py}) give, per jet, status 0 (a hadronic boson), 1 (a quark of a boson decay), 3
(another hard-process parton) or 2 (no match), with $\Delta R < 0.8$. On one Drell--Yan, one $WZ$ and one $ZH$ file the
offline match agrees with the production one for all 8329 jets. At the baseline, 54\% of the VV jets are the boson
($WZ$ about 45\%, $ZZ$ about 76\%), most of the others a hard-process parton; $ZH$: 87\%; Drell--Yan: 100\% status 3.

\paragraph{Which $\pt$ measures the boson.} With $r = (\pt^\mathrm{true} - \pt^\mathrm{reco})/\pt^\mathrm{true}$, the jet
$\pt$ estimates the boson $\pt$ better than $\ptll$ for VV and Drell--Yan (68\% half-width 0.105 vs 0.119 at
$\pt^\mathrm{true} = 200$--$300\GeV$ for VV, tails $|r| > 0.3$ half as frequent); for $ZH$ (b-jet response, neutrinos)
the two are equal. The scans agree (Table~\ref{tab:sens:binning}): for VV the jet $\pt$ is the better binning and slicing
variable (by 0.2--0.6), for $ZH$ the $Z$ $\pt$ is (2.1--2.4 vs 1.6--1.8), although the $ZH$ gain is of the size of the
scatter between stable points (0.3--0.5). In 26\% of the boson-matched $ZH$ jets (13\% for VV) the boson has
$\pt^\mathrm{true} < 200\GeV$ while the jet passes $200\GeV$; not studied further.

\paragraph{Signal jets that are not the boson.} When the selected jet of a VV event is another hard parton (55\% of $WZ$,
24\% of $ZZ$ events), the boson is soft (median $\pt$ 77 / 67\GeV for $WZ$ / $ZZ$), resolved (median $\Delta R(q,\bar q)$
2.1--2.3) and on the side of the $Z$ (median $|\Delta\phi(Z,V)|$ 0.9 / 1.7); the jet recoils against the $Z+V$ system
(median $\ptj/\ptll$ 1.14 vs 1.03 for boson jets). Only 3.8\% / 1.1\% of these events have a boson with
$\pt > 200\GeV$, $|\eta| < 2.4$ and $\Delta R(q,\bar q) < 0.8$, so a selection change cannot recover them as boosted
jets; it can only reject them. Cuts that raise the boson fraction from 0.54: no additional AK4 jet (0.83, at VV and
Drell--Yan efficiencies of 0.36 and 0.61), $0.9 < \ptj/\ptll < 1.1$ (0.66), $g_V$ at 10 / 3 / 1\% DY efficiency
(0.82 / 0.90 / 0.94). The non-boson jets are close to Drell--Yan but not identical: the $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ of the
unit-normalised shapes against Drell--Yan, per jet-$\pt$ slice from 200 to $1000\GeV$, is 3.5--7.0 for the E2C (boson
jets 32--536; two halves of Drell--Yan 0.9--1.3), 3.3--7.3 for $m_\mathrm{SD}$, 0.9--1.6 for $g_V$. Restricting both to
light-quark or to gluon jets does not remove the difference, whose cause is open. Moving them into the background lowers
the baseline VV $Z_\mathrm{shape}$ from 9.19 to 8.31 and changes it by at most 1\% at the $g_V$ working points
(13.97 to 13.86 at 1\%). Decisions (2026-11-20): these jets, and the corresponding $ZH$ ones, are background, and future
productions apply the extra-jet veto with $\alpha_\mathrm{max} = 0$ and the cuts of Section~\ref{sec:b2b}
(\code{ZX\_test}, \code{ZX\_jetfree}). On 1000 Drell--Yan $\ptll$ 400--600 test events the veto reduces the reco
\code{Zmumu} (\code{Zee}) selection from 187 to 81 (174 to 74) events, the gen one from 214 to 89 (205 to 82). The
productions used in this appendix were made without the veto.

\subsection{Particle-level production and correlators from stored particles}
\label{app:sens:particles}

\paragraph{Motivation.} Three further ideas need the particles, not the histograms: other correlators (the projected E3C,
other energy weights, the resolved E3C), other constituent choices (thresholds, vertex association, neutrals), and
correlators with no jet at all. The last follows Ref.~\cite{Generet:2026vto}, which proposes projected energy
correlators of all radiation recoiling against a $Z/\gamma$, with the boson as the hard scale and no jet algorithm,
and gives NLO+NNLL predictions for $\gamma$+X.

\paragraph{Production \code{20261119\_jetfreefull}.} Configuration \code{ZX\_jetfree} (written from \code{ZX\_test} by
\code{make\_jetfree\_config.py}): the selection of this note, histograms off, constituents written
(\code{writeConstituents}). Per channel it stores
\begin{itemize}
\item the AK8 reco and gen jets with their charged constituents (PUPPI weights applied);
\item \code{RecoEvtAllJet}: all packed PF candidates and \code{lostTracks} with $|\eta| < 2.4$, without vertex cut or
  PUPPI weighting, with \code{fromPV}, $d_z$, $d_{xy}$, the PUPPI weights and the source collection;
\item \code{GenEvtAllJet}: all stable generator particles except neutrinos with $|\eta| < 2.4$.
\end{itemize}
The selected leptons are removed from both: every PF candidate matched to a selected lepton, and for electrons also the
lost track that is the electron's CTF track (about 7\% of the selected electrons leave one, with 30--100\% of the
electron $\pt$). Generator dressing photons and leptons are matched to the packed generator particles within the
MiniAOD packing precision. On 1000 Drell--Yan test events no constituent of the lepton's flavour is left within
$\Delta R < 0.02$ of a selected lepton, the per-event weights of the unbinned correlators sum to 1 within $10^{-6}$, and
the size is 55.7\,kB per written event. Samples: Drell--Yan $\ptll$ 200--400, 400--600 and $> 600\GeV$ (MLM, 285/286,
175/180, 181/186 CRAB jobs), $WZ$ (47/47), $ZZ$ (148/149), $ZH(b\bar b)$ (7/7); the failed jobs are accepted and the
normalisation uses the jobs present.

\paragraph{Correlators offline.} Python bindings of the EEC library of this note (\code{simulation/EECs/pybind},
\code{bvana/eecext.py}) compute projected E$N$C ($N$ = 2--6, any real energy weight $n$) and the resolved E3C from the
stored particles; they reproduce the production AK8 E2C, E3C and jet-free tables within $10^{-4}$. The table key
\code{ceec} (\code{bvana/ceec.py}) adds such rows to the per-jet tables with particle filters (charge, $\pt$,
\code{fromPV}, $|d_z|$, $|d_{xy}|$ above or below a value, lost tracks, PUPPI weight or threshold, distance to the jet,
recoil hemisphere $|\Delta\phi(\cdot, Z)| > \pi/2$) and a normalisation (jet $\pt$, $Z$ $\pt$, $\sum\pt$). The AK8 E2C
rebuilt from the stored constituents equals the production row by row (relative difference $7\times10^{-8}$). Neutrals
are not stored with the AK8 jets; an AK8 jet with neutrals is approximated by all PUPPI-weighted candidates within
$\Delta R < 0.8$ of the jet axis, without lost tracks (summed weights within 1\% of the charged AK8 jet for the charged
part).

\paragraph{Resolved E3C.} For each triple with sides $R_S \le R_M \le R_L$ the coordinates are $R_L$,
$\xi = R_S/R_M$ and $\phi = \arcsin\sqrt{1 - (R_L - R_M)^2/R_S^2}$~\cite[Eq.~(3)]{Komiske:2022enw}, implemented in the
C++ library (\code{eec::triangleShape}), the bindings and a NANO producer (not enabled in any production). Triples with
a repeated particle ($R_S = 0$, where $\xi$ and $\phi$ are undefined) get $(\xi, \phi) = (-1, -1)$, the underflow of the
shape axes, which no other triple reaches; repeated particles are recognised by index, because the 3D angle of a particle
to itself is a rounding-sized number rather than 0. The tables store the $(R_L, \xi)$ and $(R_L, \phi)$ projections
(20 logarithmic $R_L$ bins $\times$ (the $R_S = 0$ column + 5 bins)).

\paragraph{Tables.} One table file per group of rows, accumulated from condor jobs and stored on EOS
(\code{/store/user/srothman/boostedV/analysis\_tables/jf\_<group>\_20261120.coffea}; sizes and checksums in the journal):
\code{ak8obs} (other correlators of the AK8 jet), \code{ak8const} (constituent choice), \code{evt} and \code{evtvtx}
(jet-free) and \code{gen} (jet-free at generator level). Drell--Yan starts at $\ptll = 200\GeV$ as before.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Neutrals.} The AK8 cone with all PUPPI-weighted particles is the best VV row at every working point
  (17.5 vs 15.1 at 3\%), stable; the charged-only cone equals the charged AK8 jet, so the gain comes from the neutrals,
  not from the cone. The jet-free PUPPI E2C, normalised to the $Z$ $\pt$, is next (16.0).
\item \textbf{Other correlators.} No other correlator of the charged AK8 jet beats the $n = 1$ E2C once the tagger is
  applied. $n = 2$ is the best at the baseline (10.1) but gains little from the tagger; the projected and resolved E3C
  are 0.5--1.3 below the E2C at the tagger working points.
\item \textbf{Constituent choice.} Particle-$\pt$ thresholds help slightly ($\pt > 2\GeV$: +0.2--0.6); vertex association
  and $d_z$ cuts lower $Z$ slightly; the PUPPI-weight threshold removes almost no energy ($\Delta Z = 0.001$).
\item \textbf{Jet-free charged and displaced rows} are far below the AK8 rows and mostly unstable: the Drell--Yan MC
  does not determine their covariance. At the baseline the displaced rows without lost tracks are higher than with
  them; at 3\% neither is consistently higher. The only stable feature for $ZH$ is the displaced row without lost tracks
  at the baseline (0.92 vs 0.27), which the b tagger then removes.
\item \textbf{$ZH$} stays below 2 at 3\% DY efficiency for every row; the largest gains over the E2C at the tagger
  working points (0.13) are inside the stable-point scatter.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reco versus generator level (jet-free).} Each level at its own baseline, slices in $\ptll$, non-boson signal
jets in the background (Table~\ref{tab:sens:genreco}). At generator level the charged-only E2C keeps most of the
sensitivity of all particles (VV 8.4 vs 9.7); at reco the charged-PV version keeps little (2.4), the PUPPI version
loses 10\% (8.8). The weakness of the charged rows is therefore a detector and pileup effect, not the restriction to
charged particles. The reco Drell--Yan shapes differ from the generator-level ones by up to about 50\% in well-measured
bins in every $\ptll$ slice. The $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ of this difference treats the two levels as independent although
they are the same events and only indicates its size. The reco PUPPI value here (8.81, $\ptll$ slices) is below the
9.39 of Table~\ref{tab:sens:particles} ($\ptj$ slices), consistent with the slicing difference of
Table~\ref{tab:sens:binning}; this was not checked with a scan in $\ptll$ slices.

\subsection{Open items}
\label{app:sens:open}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tight working points.} At $\le 1\%$ DY efficiency the MC does not determine the covariance; more
  Drell--Yan MC, a regularised covariance or a coarser fixed binning is needed before they can be judged.
\item \textbf{Theory shape for Drell--Yan} from Ref.~\cite{Generet:2026vto}: deferred.
\item \textbf{Extra-jet veto}: the studies above predate it; its effect on the sensitivity is not measured.
\item \textbf{Non-boson VV jets}: the residual difference from Drell--Yan (E2C $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ 3.5--7) and the large
  $\chi^2/\mathrm{ndf}$ (140--185) of $g_V$ and $m_\mathrm{SD}$ above $1\TeV$ (about 200 effective MC jets) are not
  understood.
\item \textbf{Resolved E3C}: only the two projections were studied; the full three-dimensional distribution is
  available offline.
\end{itemize}
