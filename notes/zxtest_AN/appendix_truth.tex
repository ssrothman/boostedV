\section{Truth spin-density matrix of the vector bosons from LHE information}
\label{app:truth}

This appendix defines the generator-level (``truth'') spin-density matrix $\rho$ of the $W$ and $Z$ bosons, derives the
estimator used to extract it from the decay angles, and documents the studies of its definition: the electroweak
couplings of the generators, the consistency of leptonic and hadronic decays, the off-shell and $\gamma^*/Z$
contributions, and the bias introduced by the event selection. The code is \code{analysis/bvana/spin.py} (formalism and
estimator), \code{analysis/truthRho.py} (extraction per boson, decay mode and $\pt$ bin) and the configuration
\code{simulation/configs/lheNano\_cfg.py}, which writes the LHE particle table and the generator weights of every event,
without any selection. The study scripts and their outputs are kept in \code{\textasciitilde/nobackup/boostedV/axisStudy\_20261118}.

\paragraph{Inputs.} Unselected LHE NANO of 24 files of \code{ZZto2L2Q} (1\,328\,453 events), 8 files of
\code{WZto2L2Q} (353\,106 events), both \textsc{Powheg-Box-V2}, and 6 files of \code{DYto2L-4Jets\_MLL-50\_PTLL-400to600}
(49\,830 events, \textsc{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO} with MLM merging). For selected events, the 149 \code{ZZto2L2Q} NANO files of
the production \code{20261116\_zxb2b}. Event weights are the signed generator weights (0.3\% of the \code{ZZto2L2Q}
weights are negative). In the LHE record the intermediate boson (status 2) and its two decay products (status 1, mother
index pointing to the boson) are identified directly; a boson is used if it has exactly two status-1 daughters.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Frame and decay angles}
\label{app:truth:frame}
For each boson $V$ with lab four-momentum $P$, both decay products are boosted into the $V$ rest frame by the pure boost
$-\vec\beta$, $\vec\beta = \vec P/P^0$, with the fermion mass of the LHE record (\textsc{Powheg} gives the quarks masses
of 0.33--4.8\GeV; treating them as massless distorts the angles of bosons of a few\GeV{} of mass,
Section~\ref{app:truth:shape}). The helicity frame has
$\hat z$ along the $V$ lab direction, $\hat x$ in the plane of $\hat z$ and the beam axis, pointing towards the beam
on the side of the boson (the beam moving along the sign of $P_z$), orthogonalised against $\hat z$, and
$\hat y = \hat z \times \hat x$. This is the convention of the \code{jetAxis} and \code{jetHelicity} frames of the E1C
(Section~\ref{sec:eec}), so that truth and jet frames coincide when the jet axis coincides with the boson. The
\emph{fermion} is the decay product with positive PDG identifier (the quark, the charged lepton $\ell^-$, or the
neutrino); $(\theta,\phi)$ are its polar and azimuthal angles in this frame. The frame is tested with hand-computed
kinematics (a boson along $+x$ at $\eta = 0$ with fermions along each axis).

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Decay distribution}
\label{app:truth:decay}
Let $\rho_{\lambda\lambda'}$, $\lambda,\lambda' \in \{+1, 0, -1\}$, be the spin-density matrix of $V$ in the helicity
basis, Hermitian, positive semi-definite, with $\operatorname{tr}\rho = 1$. In the helicity formalism of Jacob and
Wick~\cite{Jacob:1959at}, the amplitude for $V(\lambda) \to f(\lambda_1)\,\bar f(\lambda_2)$ with the fermion emitted
along $(\theta,\phi)$ in the rest frame is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}_{\lambda;\lambda_1\lambda_2}(\theta,\phi) = c_{\lambda_1\lambda_2}\, D^{1\,*}_{\lambda\sigma}(\phi,\theta,0),
  \qquad \sigma = \lambda_1 - \lambda_2,
  \qquad D^{1}_{\lambda\sigma}(\phi,\theta,0) = e^{-i\lambda\phi}\, d^{1}_{\lambda\sigma}(\theta),
  \label{eq:app:amp}
\end{equation}
where $d^1$ is the Wigner matrix in the Condon--Shortley convention and the reduced amplitudes $c_{\lambda_1\lambda_2}$
do not depend on $\lambda$ or on the angles. (Choosing $-\phi$ instead of $0$ for the third Euler angle multiplies
$\mathcal M$ by a phase that depends only on $\sigma$, which cancels in $|\mathcal M|^2$ below.) For massless fermions
the vector and axial-vector couplings conserve chirality, so $\lambda_1 = -\lambda_2$ and $\sigma = \pm1$; a
left-handed fermion ($\lambda_1 = -\tfrac12$) gives $\sigma = -1$. With the chiral couplings
$g_L = (g_V + g_A)/2$ and $g_R = (g_V - g_A)/2$, $|c_{-}|^2 \propto g_L^2$ and $|c_{+}|^2 \propto g_R^2$, so the
probabilities of the two values of $\sigma$ are
\begin{equation}
  P_{-} = \frac{g_L^2}{g_L^2 + g_R^2} = \frac{1 + A_f}{2}, \qquad P_{+} = \frac{1 - A_f}{2}, \qquad
  A_f = \frac{g_L^2 - g_R^2}{g_L^2 + g_R^2} = \frac{2 g_V g_A}{g_V^2 + g_A^2}.
  \label{eq:app:Af}
\end{equation}
Summing over the unobserved fermion helicities and normalising with $\int |d^1_{\lambda\sigma}|^2 \,d\Omega = 4\pi/3$
gives the decay distribution
\begin{equation}
  W(\theta,\phi) = \frac{3}{4\pi}\sum_{\sigma=\pm1} P_\sigma \sum_{\lambda\lambda'} \rho_{\lambda\lambda'}\,
  D^{1\,*}_{\lambda\sigma}\, D^{1}_{\lambda'\sigma}, \qquad \int W\, d\Omega = \operatorname{tr}\rho = 1.
  \label{eq:app:W}
\end{equation}
Expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:app:W} with
$d^1_{+1,\pm1} = (1\pm\cos\theta)/2$, $d^1_{0,\pm1} = \pm\sin\theta/\sqrt2$, $d^1_{-1,\pm1} = (1\mp\cos\theta)/2$
(done symbolically from the code's definition of $W$) gives
\begin{multline}
  W = \frac{3}{16\pi}\Big[ (1+\cos^2\theta)(\rho_{++}+\rho_{--}) + 2\sin^2\theta\,\rho_{00}
  - 2A_f\cos\theta\,(\rho_{++}-\rho_{--}) \\
  + 2\sin^2\theta\big(\cos2\phi\,\mathrm{Re}\,\rho_{+-} - \sin2\phi\,\mathrm{Im}\,\rho_{+-}\big) \\
  + 2\sqrt2\sin\theta\cos\theta\big(\cos\phi\,\mathrm{Re}(\rho_{+0}-\rho_{0-}) - \sin\phi\,\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{+0}-\rho_{0-})\big) \\
  - 2\sqrt2 A_f \sin\theta\big(\cos\phi\,\mathrm{Re}(\rho_{+0}+\rho_{0-}) - \sin\phi\,\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{+0}+\rho_{0-})\big)\Big].
  \label{eq:app:Wexplicit}
\end{multline}
Two checks: for a purely longitudinal boson, $W = \tfrac{3}{8\pi}\sin^2\theta$; for $\rho_{--} = 1$ and $A_f = 1$ (a $W$
boson), $W = \tfrac{3}{16\pi}(1+\cos\theta)^2$, i.e.\ the left-handed fermion follows the spin of a helicity $-1$ boson.
The terms proportional to $A_f$ are odd under $(\theta,\phi) \to (\pi - \theta, \phi + \pi)$, i.e.\ under the exchange of
fermion and antifermion (\emph{parity-odd}); the others are even. Exchanging the roles of fermion and antifermion
together with $A_f \to -A_f$ leaves $W$ unchanged, which is tested. A jet does not distinguish the quark from the
antiquark, so a jet observable symmetric under this exchange is sensitive only to the parity-even part:
$\rho_{00}$, $\rho_{+-}$ and $\rho_{+0} - \rho_{0-}$, in agreement with Ref.~\cite[App.~D]{Gao:2026bvp}: ``The energy flow is
parity even and probes the longitudinal fraction $\rho_{00}$, the alignment $\rho_{+-}$ through $\cos 2\Phi$ and
$\sin 2\Phi$, and the combination $\rho_{+0}-\rho_{0-}$ through $\cos\Phi$ and $\sin\Phi$.''

\subsection{Moment estimator}
\label{app:truth:moments}
$W$ contains only spherical harmonics of degree $l \le 2$, nine real functions in total, and $\rho$ has nine real
parameters (eight free after the trace condition). The weighted means $\langle g_k\rangle$ of the nine real functions
$g_k \in \{1, \cos\theta, \sin\theta\cos\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, 3\cos^2\theta - 1, \sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\phi,
\sin\theta\cos\theta\sin\phi, \sin^2\theta\cos2\phi, \sin^2\theta\sin2\phi\}$ are therefore linear in the parameters,
and the linear system can be inverted exactly; no fit is involved. Integrating each term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app:Wexplicit} against each $g_k$ (orthogonality of the harmonics; e.g.\
$\int P_2(\cos\theta)(1+\cos^2\theta)\,d\Omega = 8\pi/15$ and $\int P_2(\cos\theta)\,2\sin^2\theta\,d\Omega = -16\pi/15$,
so $\langle P_2\rangle = (1 - 3\rho_{00})/10$) gives
\begin{align}
  \rho_{00} &= \tfrac13\big(1 - 10\,\langle P_2(\cos\theta)\rangle\big), & \rho_{++}+\rho_{--} &= 1-\rho_{00}, \nonumber\\
  \rho_{++} - \rho_{--} &= -\frac{2}{A_f}\langle\cos\theta\rangle, & & \nonumber\\
  \mathrm{Re}\,\rho_{+-} &= \tfrac52\langle\sin^2\theta\cos2\phi\rangle, &
  \mathrm{Im}\,\rho_{+-} &= -\tfrac52\langle\sin^2\theta\sin2\phi\rangle, \label{eq:app:inversion}\\
  \mathrm{Re}(\rho_{+0}-\rho_{0-}) &= 5\sqrt2\,\langle\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\phi\rangle, &
  \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{+0}-\rho_{0-}) &= -5\sqrt2\,\langle\sin\theta\cos\theta\sin\phi\rangle, \nonumber\\
  \mathrm{Re}(\rho_{+0}+\rho_{0-}) &= -\frac{\sqrt2}{A_f}\langle\sin\theta\cos\phi\rangle, &
  \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{+0}+\rho_{0-}) &= \frac{\sqrt2}{A_f}\langle\sin\theta\sin\phi\rangle. \nonumber
\end{align}
The code builds the $9\times9$ matrix $M_{ka} = \int g_k\, W[e_a]\, d\Omega$ of the moments of each unit parameter $e_a$
numerically, with a Gauss--Legendre quadrature in $\cos\theta$ and a uniform one in $\phi$ that are exact for these
polynomials, and inverts it. The closed forms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:app:inversion} agree with $M^{-1}$ to $7\times10^{-15}$.
The statistical covariance is the weighted sample covariance of the $g_k$ (signed weights), divided by
$(\sum w)^2$ and propagated linearly. When $A_f$ varies from event to event (quark flavours), the weighted mean of $P_-$
is used, which is exact because $M$ is affine in $P_-$. Only $\rho_{++}-\rho_{--}$ and the two combinations of the last row of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app:inversion} depend on $A_f$; the parity-even ones do not depend on the
couplings at all.

\paragraph{Tests} (\code{analysis/tests/test\_spin.py}).
(i)~Normalisation and the two shapes quoted above.
(ii)~Closure: decays sampled from random valid density matrices (mixtures of two random pure states), $2\times10^5$
per test, with $P_- = 0.85$ and $0.57$, are recovered with all pulls below 4.5; using the wrong analysing power
$1 - P_-$ shifts $\rho_{++} - \rho_{--}$ by more than $5\sigma$, so the test discriminates.
(iii)~Fermion--antifermion exchange with $A_f \to -A_f$ gives the identical $\rho$.
(iv)~Off-diagonal phases: the helicity $-1$ state rotated by $+\pi/2$ about $\hat y$ (Wigner $R(0,\pi/2,0)$), which
points along $+\hat x$, gives $W = \tfrac{3}{16\pi}(1 + \hat n\cdot\hat x)^2$ for $A_f = 1$, and the estimator returns
$\mathrm{Re}\,\rho_{+0} < 0$ for it.
(v)~$A_f$ against the PDG values at $\sin^2\theta = 0.23153$ ($A_\ell = 0.147$, $A_u = 0.67$, $A_d = 0.94$).
The convention for the signs of the off-diagonal elements (the direction of $\hat x$ and the phase convention of the
helicity states) has not been compared with another extraction; the signs of $\mathrm{Re}/\mathrm{Im}$ of
$\rho_{+0}$ and $\rho_{0-}$ change with $\hat x \to -\hat x$. The diagonal elements and $\rho_{00}$ do not depend on it.

\subsection{Analysing powers and the electroweak couplings of the generators}
\label{app:truth:couplings}
At tree level, $g_A = T_3$ and $g_V = T_3 - 2Q\sin^2\theta_W$, with $T_3$ the third component of the weak isospin and
$Q$ the electric charge in units of $e$; for the $W$, $g_V = g_A$ and $A_f = 1$, as for neutrinos ($Q = 0$) from either
boson. The analysing powers of the $Z$ depend very differently on $\sin^2\theta_W$:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
$\sin^2\theta_W$ & $A_\ell$ & $A_\nu$ & $A_u$ & $A_d$ \\
\midrule
0.22263 (\textsc{Powheg} $ZZ$, $WZ$; on-shell) & 0.2164 & 1 & 0.6975 & 0.9410 \\
0.22342 (PDG on-shell $s_W^2$) & 0.2103 & 1 & 0.6949 & 0.9406 \\
0.23122 (PDG $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ $\hat s_Z^2$) & 0.1494 & 1 & 0.6685 & 0.9357 \\
0.23154 (\textsc{MadGraph} Drell--Yan; PDG effective $\bar s_\ell^2$) & 0.1469 & 1 & 0.6674 & 0.9355 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The PDG values are the Standard-Model values of Table~10.2 of the electroweak review~\cite{ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk}
(2025 update), $0.22342(9)$, $0.23122(6)$ and $0.23154(6)$; they differ by scheme (on-shell $1 - m_W^2/m_Z^2$ to
all orders, $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ at the scale $m_Z$, and the effective angle of the $Z$-pole asymmetries). At tree
level they coincide, so a leading-order generator is free to choose; the measured $Z$-pole asymmetries correspond
to the effective angle. What matters here is the value the generator used, since $\rho$ is extracted from its events.

The $W$ couples only to left-handed fermions ($g_R = 0$), so its analysing powers are 1 for every decay, independent
of $\sin^2\theta_W$ and of the CKM elements (which scale $g_L$ only). With the fermion defined as the decay product
with positive PDG identifier, this is a left-handed particle for both charges:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
decay & fermion (PDG identifier $> 0$) & $A_f$ \\
\midrule
$W^+ \to \ell^+\nu_\ell$ & $\nu_\ell$ & 1 \\
$W^- \to \ell^-\bar\nu_\ell$ & $\ell^-$ & 1 \\
$W^+ \to u_i\,\bar d_j$ & $u_i$ ($u$, $c$) & 1 \\
$W^- \to d_j\,\bar u_i$ & $d_j$ ($d$, $s$, $b$) & 1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Choosing the antifermion instead would give $A_f = -1$, equivalent by the fermion--antifermion exchange of
Section~\ref{app:truth:decay}. Fermion masses (at most $m_b$ and $m_\tau$ here) correct this by $\mathcal{O}(m_f^2/m_W^2)$
and are neglected.
For charged leptons, $g_V = -\tfrac12 + 2\sin^2\theta_W \approx -0.055$ is a near cancellation, so
$A_\ell \approx 2g_V/g_A \propto 1 - 4\sin^2\theta_W$ is a small difference of two numbers close to one: a shift of
0.009 in $\sin^2\theta_W$ changes $1 - 4\sin^2\theta_W$ from 0.110 to 0.074, i.e.\ $A_\ell$ by 47\%. For quarks,
$|Q|$ is smaller and there is no cancellation ($g_V^u \approx 0.20$, $g_V^d \approx -0.35$): $A_u$ changes by 4.5\%,
and $A_d \approx 0.94$ lies near its maximum, where it is flat (0.6\%).

The values used are those of the generators. \textsc{Powheg-Box-V2} $ZZ$ and $WZ$ (header: ``using mW, mZ. gmu
scheme, defined in ZZ/smcouplings.f'') set, in \code{ZZ/smcouplings.f} and \code{WZ/smcouplings.f} of the public
repository (last change r2479, 2013), $m_Z = 91.1876\GeV$, $m_W = 80.399\GeV$ and $G_\mu = 1.166364\times10^{-5}\GeV^{-2}$,
with $\sin^2\theta_W = 1 - m_W^2/m_Z^2 = 0.22263$; the gridpack header does not record the revision. The Drell--Yan
sample (\textsc{MadGraph} model \code{sm-ckm\_no\_b\_mass}) has, in its LHE header, $\alpha^{-1} = 128.8253$,
$G_F = 1.166379\times10^{-5}\GeV^{-2}$ and $m_Z = 91.15351\GeV$, from which the model derives
$m_W = \big[m_Z^2/2 + (m_Z^4/4 - \pi\alpha m_Z^2/(\sqrt2 G_F))^{1/2}\big]^{1/2} = 79.907\GeV$ (also printed in the
header) and $\sin^2\theta_W = 0.23154$. The \textsc{Powheg} value is the default of \code{truthRho.py}
(\code{--sin2}); for Drell--Yan, 0.23154 has to be passed. Section~\ref{app:truth:lephad} confirms the \textsc{Powheg} value
from the data themselves.

\subsection{Leptonic versus hadronic $Z$ in $ZZ\to2\ell2q$}
\label{app:truth:lephad}
In $q\bar q \to ZZ$ the two $Z$ bosons are produced symmetrically, so at the same $\pt$ the leptonically and the
hadronically decaying $Z$ have the same $\rho$; their comparison tests the formalism, the frame and the couplings, with
$A_\ell \approx 0.22$ and $A_q \approx 0.7$--$0.94$ entering the parity-odd elements very differently. The comparison
is only valid if both bosons are drawn from the same population: \code{ZZto2L2Q} is generated with
$m_{\ell\ell} > 4\GeV$ (\code{mllmin 4d0}), so the leptonic ``$Z$'' includes a large $\gamma^*$ component. If only
the boson whose $\rho$ is extracted is required in its mass window (\code{--window self}: the leptonic $Z$ when the
leptonic $\rho$ is extracted, the hadronic one for the hadronic $\rho$), the two extractions use different events, whose
partners differ (a $\gamma^*$-like partner in one case, not in the other). $\rho_{00}$ then differs between the modes
by about $20\sigma$ below $50\GeV$ ($0.272 \pm 0.003$ versus $0.203 \pm 0.002$); with both bosons required in the
window (\code{--window both}; $66 < m < 116\GeV$ for the $Z$, $60 < m < 100\GeV$ for the $W$, as in

\paragraph{Joint helicity probabilities.} The diagonal of the joint density matrix of the two bosons,
$P(\lambda_h, \lambda_\ell)$, is extracted from per-decay functions $f_\lambda(\theta)$ with $\langle f_\lambda\rangle =
\rho_{\lambda\lambda}$ for every $\rho$ (from Eq.~\eqref{eq:app:inversion}):
\begin{equation}
  f_0 = \tfrac13\big(1 - 10\,P_2(\cos\theta)\big), \qquad f_\pm = \tfrac12(1 - f_0) \mp \frac{\cos\theta}{A_f}.
\end{equation}
Being linear in $\rho$ and equal to $\rho_{\lambda\lambda}$ for every $\rho$, $f_\lambda$ is the projector onto
helicity $\lambda$; since the two decays are independent given the joint state, the weighted mean of
$f_{\lambda_h}(\theta_h)\,f_{\lambda_\ell}(\theta_\ell)$ over events is $P(\lambda_h, \lambda_\ell)$, including all spin
correlations. $A_f$ is that of each decay, so different flavours are combined exactly. The nine probabilities sum to
one, and their sum over $\lambda_\ell$ reproduces the $\rho_{\lambda\lambda}$ of Table~\ref{tab:app:truthHad} (checked to
$10^{-9}$ for $\rho_{00}$). The estimator is not constrained to be positive. A unit test samples an entangled two-boson
state with the full joint decay distribution and recovers $|\psi_{\lambda_h\lambda_\ell}|^2$, which differs from the
product of the marginals by more than $10\sigma$ (\code{analysis/tests/test\_spin.py}). Table~\ref{tab:app:truthJoint}
gives the result for the selection of Table~\ref{tab:app:truthHad}, in bins of the hadronic boson $\pt$.

\subsection{$\gamma^*/Z$ interference}
\label{app:truth:interference}
In both samples the leptonic ``$Z$'' includes $\gamma^*$ exchange and its interference with the $Z$. The photon couples
vectorially ($A = 0$), and the interference term is proportional to $Q_f\,g_A^f\,(m^2 - m_Z^2)$, with $Q_f$ the electric charge of the fermion in
units of $e$, which changes sign at
the pole; since the $Z$-only parity-odd term carries the small $g_V^\ell$ (Section~\ref{app:truth:couplings}) while the
interference carries the unsuppressed $Q_\ell g_A^\ell$, the effective leptonic analysing power must vary strongly with
$m_{\ell\ell}$. This is measured in the unselected $ZZ$ sample (538\,809 events with $Z\to ee,\mu\mu$ and a hadronic
$Z$ in its window) by comparing, in bins of $m_{\ell\ell}$, $\rho_{++}-\rho_{--}$ of the leptonic $Z$ (extracted with the
tree-level $A_\ell$) with that of the hadronic $Z$ of the same events. Since both bosons have the same $\rho$, the ratio
is $(A_\ell^\mathrm{eff}(m_{\ell\ell})/A_\ell^\mathrm{tree})\,(A_q^\mathrm{tree}/A_q^\mathrm{eff})$, which is flat
without interference:

varying across the window, would not cancel. The hadronic side is affected much less (larger $g_V^q$, smaller $|Q_q|$)
and is not separately measured here. The jet observables are parity-even, so none of this affects them.

\subsection{Bias from the event selection}
\label{app:truth:bias}
Selected events cannot be used as the truth, because the selection depends on the decay angles. The size and the
source of this bias were measured on the hadronic $Z$ of \code{ZZto2L2Q}: $\rho$ from the selected events of the
production \code{20261116\_zxb2b} (149 NANO files, which store the LHE particles) is compared with $\rho$ from the
independent unselected LHE files, in bins of the LHE boson $\pt$.

\paragraph{Containment.} For a boson of mass $m$ and energy $E \gg m$ decaying to two massless partons with energy
fractions $z$ and $1-z$, $m^2 = 2E^2 z(1-z)(1-\cos\theta_{12}) \approx E^2 z(1-z)\,\theta_{12}^2$, and
$z = (1 + \beta\cos\theta^*)/2$ with $\theta^*$ the rest-frame decay angle, so $z(1-z) \approx \sin^2\theta^*/4$ and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta R(q,\bar q) \approx \frac{2m}{\pt\,\sin\theta^*}.
  \label{eq:app:containment}
\end{equation}
Even symmetric decays ($\theta^* = \pi/2$) are contained in an $R = 0.8$ jet only for $\pt \gtrsim 2m/R \approx
230\GeV$ ($Z$) or $200\GeV$ ($W$), and asymmetric ones need more. The fraction of unselected hadronic $Z$ decays with
$\Delta R(q,\bar q) < 0.8$, in bins of $|\cos\theta^*|$ (0--0.2, \ldots, 0.8--1), is 0.56, 0.46, 0.27, 0.07, 0.00 at
200--300\GeV; 1.00, 1.00, 0.96, 0.65, 0.09 at 300--400\GeV; and 1.00, 1.00, 1.00, 0.97, 0.36 at 400--600\GeV.

\paragraph{Two-prong requirements.} Requiring both quarks inside the jet (C2), or a soft-drop mass window (C3), biases
$\rho_{00}$ upwards by 0.4--1.2 at every $\pt$, including above $600\GeV$ for the mass window. With
$z_\mathrm{cut} = 0.1$ and $\beta = 0$, soft drop removes decays in which the softer prong carries less than 10\% of the
$\pt$; since $z \approx (1+\cos\theta^*)/2$, this is $|\cos\theta^*| > 1 - 2z_\mathrm{cut} = 0.8$, the bin whose
acceptance drops to 0.13--0.20. Any groomed-mass window, or a tagger relying on it, therefore cuts directly into the
polarisation-sensitive region. This is a property of such a selection, not of the thresholds, and has to be taken
into account in the design of the measurement (avoiding such requirements, or unfolding through them with the full
angular response).

\subsection{Reweighting to pure longitudinal or transverse bosons}
\label{app:truth:reweighting}
The axis study of Appendix~\ref{app:axes} needs the response of the observables to $\rho_{00}$. Each selected jet
matched to an LHE hadronic boson is reweighted by the polar distribution of the quark, symmetrised in quark and
antiquark (so that the parity-odd term drops), relative to the unselected truth $\rho_{00}$ of its boson $\pt$ bin:
\begin{equation}
  f(c) = (1-\rho_{00})\,\tfrac38(1+c^2) + \rho_{00}\,\tfrac34(1-c^2), \qquad
  w_L = \frac{\tfrac34(1-c^2)}{f(c)}, \qquad w_T = \frac{\tfrac38(1+c^2)}{f(c)}, \qquad c = \cos\theta^*.
\end{equation}
Since the acceptance multiplies numerator and denominator alike, it cancels in the ratio. On unselected LHE events (23
\code{ZZto2L2Q} files, 17 November) the reweighting gives $\rho_{00} = 1.005 \pm 0.005$ ($w_L$) and
$0.008 \pm 0.008$ ($w_T$). The parity-odd and off-diagonal elements are not removed: $\rho_{++} - \rho_{--} = -0.12$
remains in the ``pure longitudinal'' sample, which is acceptable for comparing axes but does not make it a pure
state. Better options, in order of exactness: a dedicated generation with polarised intermediate bosons in
\textsc{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO}~\cite{BuarqueFranzosi:2019boy} (exact, including the correlations with the rest of the
event, but a different generator from the nominal one); a per-event reweighting with helicity amplitudes evaluated on
the LHE kinematics (exact per event, without new events, but substantial work); and a reweighting by the ratio of the
full decay distributions $W_\mathrm{pure}/W_\mathrm{true}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:app:Wexplicit} with all nine truth elements
(removes the residual elements on average per bin; finite, since $W_\mathrm{true} > 0$). None is implemented yet.
