# 1203.2165 — Measurement of the polarisation of W bosons produced with large transverse momentum in pp collisions at √s = 7 TeV with the ATLAS experiment

- Authors/Collaboration: ATLAS Collaboration. Year: not in the fetched text (arXiv ID implies 2012). Journal: Eur. Phys. J. C (volume/pages not in fetched text). INSPIRE recid 1093486. HEPData: none (record URL returned 404; HEPData search for arXiv:1203.2165 returned 0 hits).
- √s = 7 TeV; integrated luminosity 37.3 pb⁻¹ (electron channel), 31.4 pb⁻¹ (muon channel), text quotes "about 35 pb-1". Data (2010), with mc@nlo and powheg simulation as predictions.
- Topology / final state: W → eν and W → μν (inclusive W production, pp).
- Selection summary:
  - Trigger: single-muon p_T^ℓ ≥ 13 GeV or single-electron p_T^ℓ ≥ 15 GeV (Sec. 3.4) [verifier w1_5].
  - Corrected-result acceptance: |η_ℓ| < 2.4, p_T^ν > 25 GeV, p_T^ℓ > 20 GeV, 50 < m_T^W < 110 GeV (Sec. 10) [verifier w1_5].
  - W p_T bins: 35 < p_T^W < 50 GeV and p_T^W > 50 GeV (Abstract, Sec. 1).
  - Measured variable: cos θ_2D ("transverse helicity" angle), built from the lepton p_T in the transverse W rest frame and the W p_T in the lab, Eq. (6), with the neutrino p_T identified with MET (Sec. 2.3, Sec. 10) [verifier w1R_b]; the recoil enters only as an energy-scale systematic (Sec. 8.5).
  - Jet algorithm and R: not extracted (not stated in the parts of the text read).
- Model or observable definition (with frame/convention):
  - Lepton angular distribution, Eq. 1: dσ/(d(p_T^W)² dy_W dcosθ dφ) with coefficients A_0 … A_7; θ, φ are the lepton polar and azimuthal angles "in a given W rest frame" (Sec. 2.1). A_5–A_7 neglected.
  - Helicity frame (Sec. 2.2): "The helicity frame is the W rest frame with the z -axis along the W laboratory direction of flight and the x -axis in the event plane, in the hemisphere opposite to the recoil system."
  - Helicity-fraction relations, Eq. 4: f_L = (1/4)(2 − A_0 ∓ A_4), f_R = (1/4)(2 − A_0 ± A_4), f_0 = (1/2)A_0; "where the upper (lower) sign corresponds to W+ (W−) boson production respectively."
  - Eq. 5: f_L − f_R = ∓A_4/2 (same sign convention as Eq. 4).
  - Measured quantities: f_0 and f_L − f_R (the paper states f_L and f_R individually are not the independent measured variables; Sec. 10). Averaging stated as "averaged over charge" / "averaged over charges and lepton flavours" (Table 8, Table 11).
  - Reference frame for the corrected values: generator-level "true" cos θ_3D fits within the acceptance cuts (Sec. 8.6, Sec. 9).

## Dominant systematics (as stated by the paper, with location)

- Recoil energy scale (Sec. 8.5): "This is the largest systematic uncertainty on the helicity fractions measured in this study. In the worst case (muons in the low p_T^W bin), the resulting uncertainty on f_0 is 16%." Table 7 lists the recoil-scale entries per bin/flavour; the Table 7 column mapping was not verified, so no Table 7 values are quoted here.
- Lepton energy scale (Sec. 8.5): "The resulting uncertainties are about 3% to 5% on f_0 and around 2% on f_L-f_R."
- Choice of MC generator / templates (Sec. 8.6, Table 8): "the systematic uncertainty associated with the choice of templates was taken as half the difference between the two numbers, with a minimum value of 2%." Quoted as "template" in the Table 8 entries above. Also stated: "The systematic uncertainty associated with the differences between the two sets of templates is large for f_0, for which other systematic effects are also large."
- Other systematics listed in Table 7 (EW background, jet background, PDF set, charge mis-ID, p_T^ℓ resolution, p_T^W reweighting): entries not quoted individually; Table 7 column mapping not verified.
- Stat/syst/other breakdown of the combined Table 11 systematic: "the second covers all systematic uncertainties."

## Caveats

- Measurement is the data (2010, 35 pb⁻¹); predictions are mc@nlo and powheg, with statistical uncertainties only (Table 11 caption).
- Results are corrected to the generator-level acceptance, using 3D-template fits as the reference: "the raw results from Section 7 are corrected by adding the difference, found using simulations, between the 'true' values which would be given by fits to cos θ_3D distributions obtained at the generator level within acceptance and m_T^W cuts as used here" (Sec. 8.6).
- The longitudinal fraction is hard to extract: "The longitudinal fraction is the most difficult to extract and has rather large systematic uncertainties, especially in the low p_T^W bin, mostly associated with the recoil energy scale and with the choice of Monte Carlo generator." (Sec. 10).
- Interpretation: "in the high p_T^W bin, the data favour f_0 values smaller than the predictions of mc@nlo and powheg, which are close to each other." (Sec. 9) [verifier w1_5]. Authors add: "Due to the large uncertainties on the measurements, however, no stringent constraints nor clear inconsistencies can be deduced." (Sec. 9) [verifier w1_5].
- Sec. 9: "For p_T^W > 50 GeV, the data are close to the mc@nlo values, while powheg predicts a somewhat smaller difference between left- and right-handed states than observed in the data."
- Charge averaging: f_0 systematics from energy scales do not cancel between charges; the averaging is stated as done "to reduce the statistical uncertainties ... and the uncorrelated instrumental and analysis systematic uncertainties" (Sec. 9).
- Sherpa cross-check (electron only, Table 9): reported as "very good agreement"; Table 9 values not extracted.
