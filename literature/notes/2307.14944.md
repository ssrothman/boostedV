# 2307.14944 — Pursuit of paired dijet resonances in the Run 2 dataset with ATLAS

- Authors/Collaboration: ATLAS Collaboration; 2023; Phys. Rev. D 108 (2023) 112005 (arXiv v2, 10 Jan 2024; CERN-EP-2023-147); INSPIRE recid 2682337; HEPData record 140530 (DOI 10.17182/hepdata.140530.v1), limit tables 25–72 used.
- sqrt(s) = 13 TeV; integrated luminosity 140 fb⁻¹ (2015–2018 data; 0.83% luminosity uncertainty, Sec. 2.2 and 3.5); data. Signal shapes and acceptance from Pythia 8.230 simulation (Sec. 2.3).
- Topology / final state: pp → Y → X X, each X → two jets; final state of four jets, reconstructed as two dijet pairs (fully hadronic). Y spin and parity: not stated.
- Selection summary:
  - Anti-kt R = 0.4 particle-flow jets, pT > 60 GeV, |y| < 2.4, at least four jets (Sec. 3.1–3.2).
  - Dijet pairing minimizes ΔR = |ΔR_AB − 0.8| + |ΔR_CD − 0.8|; requires ΔR_AB, ΔR_CD < 2.0, Δη < 1.1 and a mass asymmetry between the pairs (Sec. 3.2).
  - Observables m_4j (proxy for m_Y) and ⟨m_2j⟩ (proxy for m_X); α = ⟨m_2j⟩/m_4j in twelve bins covering 0.10 < α < 0.34 (Sec. 3.2).
  - Single-jet triggers; per-α minimum ⟨m_2j⟩ and m_4j thresholds (Table 1), e.g. 230 GeV and 1775 GeV for 0.10 < α < 0.12.
  - Background: 4-parameter dijet function in 1 GeV bins; BumpHunter for local excesses (Sec. 4.1).
- Model or observable definition (frame/convention):
  - Signal MC: Pythia 8.230 W′ → WZ with m_W′ = m_Y and m_W = m_Z = m_X; the W and Z widths are 0.1 GeV, X is forced to qq̄, and top-pair decays are disabled (Sec. 2.3).
  - Model-dependent limits: Crystal Ball signal shape fitted to simulated signal, with interpolation between mass points (Sec. 3.3).
  - Model-independent limits: Gaussian signal templates with mean m_Y (or m_X) and width 5%, 10%, 15% of the mass (Sec. 3.3, Fig. 6).
  - Limit form: 95% CL upper limit on σ × A × ε × BR in pb (HEPData header; Sec. 4.2). Not a coupling g_q limit.
  - Polarization / reference frame: no polarization observable; frame not stated.
  - CL method: modified frequentist CLs, in an asymptotic approximation to the test-statistic distribution (Sec. 4.2). The test statistic named by the paper is the profile-likelihood-ratio, used for the local p0 (Sec. 4.2); the limit test statistic is not named.

## Dominant systematics (as stated by the paper, with location)

- Jet energy scale: "the most significant source of uncertainty in the JES now originates from the absolute in situ JES calibration" (Sec. 3.5, Jet energy scale and resolution). No numeric effect on the limit is given.
- Background modeling: S_modeling, "determined as the envelope of |S_spur| over m4j and ⟨m2j⟩ respectively", implemented as an additional signal contribution (Sec. 3.5, Background modeling). No numeric value given in the text.
- Luminosity: 0.83%, "treated as a single normalization uncertainty applied as a scale factor to the signal models" (Sec. 3.5, Luminosity).
- PDF (NNPDF 2.3 LO): "This uncertainty is a sub-1% effect for all signal models considered." (Sec. 3.5, Parton distribution functions).
- Renormalization/factorization scales: "typically less than 0.5%" on the mean of the signal distribution (Sec. 3.5).
- α_S (A14 eigentune): "The impact of this variation is negligible compared with other systematic variations." (Sec. 3.5).
- Statistical vs systematic: "The analysis sensitivity was not observed to significantly differ during this test." (Sec. 4.2, stat-only limit test). No numeric stat/syst split of the limits.

## Caveats

- Limits are on σ × A × ε × BR for a generic Y → XX → 4j topology, with the signal generated as W′ → WZ (Sec. 2.3). They are not limits on a narrow-resonance coupling g_q, and Y spin/parity is not stated.
- Mass reach: over all m_Y tables, 2000–10000 GeV (the lower edge differs per table: 2000 to 5000 GeV); over all m_X tables, 500–3300 GeV (Sec. 3.2 acceptance statement, "500 GeV < m_X < 3300 GeV"). Within 10 GeV–1 TeV only m_X at 500–1000 GeV is covered (Section A). No m_Y point is in the primary window.
- Acceptance × efficiency: "between 12%–45%" as a function of the signal masses (Sec. 3.2). This is text, not a limit.
- Interpolation: "Results are interpolated linearly in the logarithm of the cross-section." (Sec. 4.2).
- Observed limits are not monotonic in some tables, e.g. Table 25 (m_Y, 0.10 < α < 0.12): 0.00005616 at 5000 GeV, 0.00008720 at 5500 GeV, 0.00003863 at 6000 GeV. Read the HEPData values, not a trend.
- Low-mass plateaus: Table 44 has observed 0.00217051 at m_X = 500, 600, 700 GeV, with expected 0.00164039 at the same three points; Table 46 has observed 0.00158092 and expected 0.00118066 at m_X = 600, 700, 800 GeV. The paper does not discuss these (Open issue 3).
- High-mass behaviour: the limits "flatten out in the high-mass region where the background estimation predicts significantly less than one event" (Sec. 4.2).
- Search result: the paper reports its most significant BumpHunter local excesses at 3200 GeV (global significance 0.53 standard deviations) and at 800 GeV (global significance 1.98 standard deviations) (Sec. 4.1) [verifier w2_2: spectrum and α of each excess not recorded; "no significant excess" is not stated in Sec. 4.1, removed].

## Open issues

1. Observed/expected mapping: HEPData labels group 0 "Observed" and group 1 "Fit" (Gaussian: "Observed sigma/M = 0.05/0.10/0.15" and "Fit sigma/M = ..."). The "Fit" group is mapped to expected because the bands sit only on that group, and the Fig. 5 caption says "Observed and expected limits are indicated with markers or a dashed line, respectively" with bands around the expected limit. HEPData does not itself say "expected". Confirm before relying on the labels.
2. Figure labels: HEPData location fields cite Figures 4a–4l, 5a–5l, 6a–6l, 7a–7l, and Table 56 cites "Figure 14h" (its associated file says "Figure 6h"). The paper's own numbering differs (Fig. 5 panels a, c, e are m_Y and b, d, f are m_X). The "HEPData Table N" name is used as the location throughout. Not resolved.
3. Low-mass plateaus at m_X 500–700 GeV (Table 44) and 600–800 GeV (Table 46) are identical in both observed and expected values across those points. Not explained in the text; not investigated further.
4. Primary-window scope: the m_Y (resonance) tables have no point in 10 GeV–1 TeV. Section A includes the m_X rows (X is the intermediate resonance), as the scope rule requests. Whether m_X rows count for this question is a scoping decision for the user.
5. Quotes: the verbatim quotes are built from the HEPData JSON fields in the form "x=… :: gN=value [label:plus/minus]". They are not copied from the HEPData web page. Every value was taken from the cached JSON, not from plots (no figure-only values were used).
6. Text sources: the cached arXiv HTML (`.cache/2307.14944/arxiv_html.html`, arXiv v2) and the plain-text extraction (`paper.txt`) were used; math symbols were read from the HTML's TeX annotations. The arXiv fetch in step 1 was not repeated. Table 1 bin labels and the Fig. 5/6 captions were read from the HTML.
7. Section B lists endpoints and round reference masses only, per the scope rule. Full rows are in the cached HEPData JSON (`.cache/2307.14944/hepdata/tables/`).
8. Table 73 and 74 (acceptance and signal cross-section) and all figure-only values were not extracted.
9. Gaussian bands: HEPData lists bands only for σ/M = 0.05 (group 1 of each table). For σ/M = 0.10 and 0.15 the JSON error entries are hidden and have symerror 0; these are written as "no band listed", not as zero.
