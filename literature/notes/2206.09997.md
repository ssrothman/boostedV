# 2206.09997 — Search for resonant and nonresonant production of pairs of dijet resonances in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV

- Authors/Collaboration: CMS Collaboration (HEPData: "Tumasyan, Armen et al."); journal year 2023; arXiv posted 2022-06-20 (HEPData creation date).
- Journal ref: JHEP 07 (2023) 161, DOI 10.1007/JHEP07(2023)161 (from HEPData record.json, `journal_info`). Preprint arXiv:2206.09997; EXO-21-010.
- INSPIRE recid: 2098256. HEPData record: recid 130817 (INSPIRE ins2098256), DOI 10.17182/hepdata.130817.v1; cached locally, 19 data tables.
- sqrt(s) = 13 TeV; integrated luminosity 138 fb^-1 (2016–2018) (Sec. 0.1: "corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 138\fbinv"); measured data, not projection.
- Topology / final state: all-hadronic four-jet. (a) Resonant: pp → Y → X X → (jj)(jj), benchmark Y = scalar diquark decaying to two vector-like quarks, each → q g (Sec. 0.1, Eqs. 1–2). (b) Nonresonant: pp → X X → (jj)(jj), benchmark X = top squark with RPV decays to d and s quarks (Sec. 0.1, Eqs. 3–4).
- Selection summary:
  - Anti-kT R = 0.4 PF jets, pT > 80 GeV, |η| < 2.5; four leading jets; jet-ID applied; any of the four leading jets failing ID rejects the event (Sec. 0.4).
  - Pairing: minimize |ΔR1 − 0.8| + |ΔR2 − 0.8| over three pairings (Eq. 6); then ΔR1,2 < 2.0 (Eq. 7), |Δη| < 1.1 (Eq. 8), dijet asymmetry < 0.1 (Eq. 9).
  - Variables: α = m̄jj / m4j. Resonant search: m4j fit in 13 α bins (0.1–0.34 in 0.02 steps plus overflow), m4j > 1.6 TeV. Nonresonant search: m̄jj fit in 3 α bins (0.15–0.25, 0.25–0.35, overflow), with m̄jj thresholds 0.35, 0.53, 0.61 TeV (Sec. 0.5).
  - Trigger: L1 single-jet plus HLT; searches start above the mass thresholds where the trigger is fully efficient (Sec. 0.5).
  - Background: data-driven fit of ModDijet-3p (primary), Dijet-3p and PowExp-3p (alternatives), discrete profiling of the functional form (Sec. 0.7).
- Model or observable definition (frame/convention):
  - Limit observable: σ·B·A, "the product of the cross section, branching fraction, and acceptance" (Figs. 10–12). Sec. 0.6: "Cross section upper limits in this paper are quoted within this acceptance and corrected for efficiency." Branching fraction for the benchmark not separately stated.
  - Resonant mass axis: "the limits are presented as a function of the four-jet resonance mass between 2 and 9\TeV" (Summary). HEPData column "Resonance mass [GeV]" is therefore read as M(Y); the HEPData header itself does not say which (see Open issues). α_true = M(X)/M(Y), one table per α_true value.
  - Nonresonant mass axis: "functions of dijet resonance mass between 0.5 and 3.0\TeV" (Summary); read as the top-squark (X) mass.
  - Benchmark couplings: diquark y_uu = 0.4 and y_χ = 0.6 (Sec. 0.8); "scalar diquark" (Sec. 0.1).
  - Frame / polarization: not applicable; the paper measures no angular or polarization observable. No f_L, f_0, A_0, ρ_00 or reference frame is stated.
  - CL: "Model-independent upper limits at 95% confidence level (\CL)"; method: "The modified frequentist criterion [65, 68] is used to set upper limits on the signal cross sections ... using the asymptotic approximation of the test statistic" (Sec. 0.8), with pseudo-experiments where counts are small.

## Dominant systematics (as stated by the paper, with location)

All from Sec. 0.8 (Results) unless noted:
- Jet energy scale: "The uncertainty in the jet energy scale is below 2% for all values of the four-jet and dijet masses." Propagated as ±2% shifts of the signal mass shapes; Gaussian nuisance.
- Jet energy resolution: "translates into an uncertainty of 10% in the resolution of the four-jet and dijet masses"; propagated by a 10% widening of signal mass shapes; Gaussian nuisance.
- Integrated luminosity: "The total integrated luminosity has an uncertainty of 1.6%," applied to signal normalization; log-normal nuisance.
- Stated ranking: "The dominant sources of systematic uncertainty in the signal modeling are the jet energy scale, the jet energy resolution, and the integrated luminosity." and "The uncertainties with the largest impact on the extracted signal yield are the ones relating to background, with the signal modeling related ones being negligible."
- Background: "The main source of the uncertainty in the background modeling comes from the choice of the background function, and the range of possible values of the parameters of the background function." Handled by discrete profiling of the functional form (Sec. 0.8).
- No separate statistical-only vs systematic breakdown of the limits is given.

## Caveats

- Observed vs expected: "the observed limit is more than 2 standard deviations (s.d.) above the expected limit" at ~0.95 TeV in the nonresonant search (Sec. 0.8, Fig. 12); the paper links this to low background-fit p-values there. Resonant search: "the observed limit is also more than 2 s.d. above the expected limit for a four-jet resonance mass between 3 and 4\TeV" for α_true = 0.27, 0.29, 0.31 (Sec. 0.8).
- Background fit p-values: resonant "The combined pp-value is 0.18" (Sec. 0.7); nonresonant "the combined pp-value ... is 0.04" (Sec. 0.7).
- Excesses (not limits): resonant "the local and global significance are 3.9 s.d. and 1.6 s.d., respectively" at four-jet mass 8.6 TeV, α_true = 0.25 (Sec. 0.8); nonresonant "the local significance is 3.6 s.d. and the global significance is 2.5 s.d." at 0.95 TeV (Sec. 0.8). Both are signal interpretations, with two events in the resonant tail (Sec. 0.9).
- Signal cross-section theory used for the diquark mass limits: NLO with NNPDF NLO 3.1 for diquark masses above 6 TeV; below 6 TeV "a \MGvATNLOcalculation multiplied by a KK factor of 1.47" (Sec. 0.8). Mass limits depend on the coupling choice: "Other choices of coupling values can yield different values for the diquark cross sections and the mass limits [58]."
- Mass limits quoted are for the single benchmark couplings y_uu = 0.4, y_χ = 0.6 (Sec. 0.8).
- Acceptance: "The acceptance ... is approximately independent of the resonance mass in both searches" (Sec. 0.6); limits are in σ·B·A units, so they are not directly cross-section limits without the acceptance.
- The resonant search covers 1.6–10 TeV in evaluation (Sec. 0.8 "from 1.6 to 10\TeV in 0.1\TeV steps") but the paper presents 2–9 TeV (Summary); HEPData has 2000.0–9000.0 GeV only.
- Pairing efficiency for the nonresonant search falls "from 0.4 at the highest top squark mass to 1.0 at the lowest mass" (Sec. 0.6).
