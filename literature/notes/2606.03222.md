# 2606.03222 — Search for electroweak scale dijet resonances in pile-up collisions at √s = 13 TeV with the ATLAS detector

- Authors/Collaboration: ATLAS Collaboration (INSPIRE lists 2863 authors); 2026 (arXiv:2606.03222v1, 02 Jun 2026, CERN-EP-2026-147); journal ref: preprint (the TeX macro `\AtlasJournalEPJ C` is present, but INSPIRE `publication_info` is empty); INSPIRE recid 3164942; HEPData: ins3164942 (exists).
- √s = 13 TeV; integrated luminosity 1.30 pb⁻¹ (effective luminosity of the pile-up dataset, 2016–2018; not the delivered luminosity). Data, with MC simulation for signal templates and a background-only fit to data.
- Topology / final state: resolved dijet, two leading jets reconstructed from pile-up primary vertices (PPVs) in bunch crossings recorded by single-electron/single-muon triggers. Interpreted for a Z′ → quark pairs (u, d, s, c, b) and for generic Gaussian resonances (final state not restricted; paper notes gluon jets can contribute).
- Selection summary:
  - Trigger/RBC: single-electron or single-muon trigger with 26 GeV HLT threshold; RBC vetoed if no TPV can be found; PPVs within the TPV veto window are removed: `the TPV is vetoed together with any primary vertices within` `\left|\Delta z(\mathrm{TPV,PV})\right|<2\mathrm{\ mm}` (Sec. 3, 9).
  - Jets: anti-kt R = 0.4 on particle-flow objects, run once per PPV; per PPV at least two jets with p_T > 17 GeV and |η| < 2.0, JVT applied (Sec. 7.2, 9).
  - PPV-level isolation: jets from different PPVs within ΔR = 0.4 veto the PPV; the isolation step lowers the jet threshold to 15 GeV and extends |η| from 2.0 to 2.4 (Sec. 9). Also Tile-gap cleaning and a jet-timing requirement: `be within 10 ns of the nominal bunch-crossing time` (Sec. 9).
  - Rapidity: `the two leading jets in each PPV are required to satisfy the condition` `\left|y^{*}\right|=\frac{\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|}{2}<0.8` (Sec. 9).
  - Fit range m_jj 55–300 GeV; signal limits only for 100–250 GeV (Sec. 11.1, 11.2).
- Model or observable definition (with frame/convention):
  - Z′: s-channel leptophobic axial-vector mediator, equal coupling g_q to all five light/heavy quark flavours; grid m_Z′ = 80, 100, 150, 250 GeV and g_q ∈ [0.2, 1.0] in steps of 0.2 (g_q > 1.0 not considered, width/mass > 50%). Intrinsic width included; no interference with SM Z; decays to dark matter neglected (`with the dark matter coupling set to $g_{\chi}=1$`) (Sec. 3). Signal generated at LO with MadGraph 2.9.16 `at leading order (LO) in perturbative QCD` (Sec. 3).
  - Gaussian: mean = resonance mass, relative total width σ_G/m_G = 10, 15, 20, 25% (intrinsic + detector) (Sec. 11.2).
  - Observable: σ×A×B, cross section × acceptance × efficiency × branching ratio to jets. Z′ coupling limits obtained by translating σ×A×B limits with the generated Z′ cross sections (Sec. 14).
  - Limit method: `A modified frequentist approach [64] is used to derive upper limits at 95% CL with the $\mathrm{CL}_{\mathrm{S}}$ [65] technique for each signal scenario.` (Sec. 14).
  - Frame / polarization quantities: not stated; no polarization or spin-correlation observable is measured (not applicable).

## Dominant systematics (as stated by the paper, with location)

- Jet energy scale and resolution: 43 nuisance parameters, `An ensemble of 43 nuisance parameters are considered` (Sec. 12). Largest effect on the signal mean: `The largest jet-energy-related sources of uncertainty on the mean of the signal are the jet flavour composition and response, with differences up to 2 GeV.` (Sec. 12). Width impact: sub-GeV, negligible (Sec. 12).
- Spurious-signal (background-modelling) uncertainty: `with maximum amplitude $0.5\sigma_{\mathrm{spur}}$ and constrained by Gaussian terms in the likelihood.` (Sec. 12).
- Luminosity: `the luminosity of the collected pile-up dataset is 0.83%.` Stated as negligible relative to the other experimental sources (Sec. 12).
- Pile-up reweighting: varied via the total inelastic cross section; size not quantified in the text (Sec. 12).
- Lepton-related TPV-removal efficiencies: stated as negligible (Sec. 12).
- Statistical vs systematic split of the limits, and whether the expected bands include statistical uncertainties: not stated.

## Caveats

- Mass coverage: limits only for 100–250 GeV (`Only signals with resonance masses between 100 and 250 GeV are considered in the upper limit computation`, Sec. 11.1). No BSM interpretation below 100 GeV because the W/Z template is not precisely estimated (Sec. 11.1).
- Luminosity: `corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 1.30 pb-1, which represents the effective luminosity of pile-up collisions recorded alongside triggered events.` (Abstract). Limits are for the pile-up dataset only.
- Signal modelling: generated at LO; `The signal samples neglect any interference effects between the $Z^{\prime}$ and the SM $Z$ boson.` (Sec. 3). Theory line: `The theory line represents the expected $\sigma\times A\times B$ estimated from the generated signal samples.` (Fig. 4 caption).
- Signal shape: DSCB fit to templates; `The mean of the Gaussian bulk was found to be shifted downwards by around 6–7% compared to the generated $Z^{\prime}$ mass.` (Sec. 11.1).
- Background: fit with f_fit(x, N=5) selected by F-test; `The fitted template describes the data well, with a $\chi^{2}$ $p$ -value of 0.08` (Sec. 14). Fit range: `The $m_{jj}$ range used in the fit spans from 55 to 300 GeV.` (Sec. 11.2).
- Local excess: `corresponding to a local significance of 3.0 $\sigma$` at m_Z′ = 112 GeV, g_q = 0.6 or 0.8 (Sec. 14); `this corresponds to a global significance of 2.3 $\sigma$` after look-elsewhere (Sec. 14, pseudodata).
- BumpHunter (shape-independent scan): `The largest excess is found between 106 and 131 GeV, with a local significance of 1.7 $\sigma$` (Sec. 14); `The corresponding global $p$ -value of 0.67` (Sec. 14). The paper states this global p-value is distinct from the CLs global significance.
- Z′ width: `The intrinsic width of the $Z^{\prime}$ is accounted for at all times, and is found to be non-negligible for $g_{q}$ larger than roughly 0.2` (Sec. 3). No numerical widths are tabulated, so narrow-width status is not established. Gaussian templates have 10–25% relative width, i.e. are not narrow.
- Comparison: `While more stringent limits are obtained for the $Z^{\prime}$ model using boosted techniques` (Sec. 14, boosted dijet+ISR search, ref. [17]).
