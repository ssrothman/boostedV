# 1911.03761 — Search for dijet resonances using events with three jets in proton-proton collisions at sqrt(s) = 13 TeV

- CMS Collaboration (first author Sirunyan, Albert M), 2020; preprint arXiv:1911.03761 v2 (30 Apr 2020), CMS-EXO-19-004, CERN-EP-2019-229; journal Phys. Lett. B 805 (2020) 135448; INSPIRE recid 1764796; HEPData record 91058 (ins1764796), version 1, DOI 10.17182/hepdata.91058.v1.
- sqrt(s) = 13 TeV; integrated luminosity 18.3 fb^-1 (2016 data, data-scouting trigger); data.
- Topology / final state: narrow s-channel dijet resonance (qq → V → qq̄) in events with at least three jets; the third jet is ISR/FSR. Fully hadronic. Singly produced (paper intro: "qq → qq, s-channel"). Associated production with ISR jet is inherent to the three-jet selection.
- Selection summary:
  - Trigger: L1 H_T > 240 GeV and HLT H_T > 250 GeV, H_T = scalar sum of jets with pT > 40 GeV and |η| < 2.5; data scouting, 2016 only (Sec. 3).
  - HLT calo jets, anti-kT R = 0.4, pT > 30 GeV, |η| < 2.5; ECAL and HCAL each carry ≥ 5% of jet energy (Sec. 4).
  - Wide jets: anti-kT with R = 1.1 recombination; at least three wide jets with pT > 72 GeV and |η| < 2.5; the two leading wide jets have |η1 − η2| < 1.1 (Sec. 4).
  - Dijet system = two leading wide jets (largest and next-largest pT); fit range 290 < m_jj < 1000 GeV (Sec. 5, Fig. 1 caption). Search limits presented for 350–700 GeV (Sec. 7).
  - Background: analytic function Eq. (1) with five free parameters; fit range and 72 GeV threshold chosen with a signal-depleted region (|η1 + η2| < 1.1) and KS test > 33% (Sec. 5).
- Model or observable definition (with frame/convention):
  - Observable: dijet invariant mass m_jj of the two leading wide jets.
  - Signal: narrow vector resonance decaying to qq̄, generated with MadGraph5_aMC@NLO (LO), width negligible vs ~10% mass resolution (Sec. 3). Upper limits on σ×B×A (Fig. 2) and on the universal quark coupling g'_q (Fig. 3) for a vector coupling only to quarks, convention of Ref. [89] (Sec. 7).
  - Coupling conversion between g_q and g'_q is given by Eq. (2) with N_q from Eq. (3) (Sec. 7); not reproduced here. HEPData g'_q values are used as given.
  - DM benchmark (not a limit): g_q = 0.25, g_DM = 1, m_DM = 1 GeV (Sec. 3, Sec. 7). Dashed-dotted curves in Figs. 2–3 are predictions, not limits.
  - Polarization / reference frame: not applicable (no polarization observable in the paper).
  - Limit method: asymptotic approximation of the modified frequentist CLs, profiled likelihood-ratio test statistic, Poisson likelihood per bin of Fig. 1 (Sec. 6).
  - Frame / convention notes: the paper writes the quark-only coupling as "gq0" in the text extraction; Fig. 3 caption and HEPData use g'_q.

## Dominant systematics (as stated by the paper, with location)

- Background-shape fit (five profiled parameters of Eq. (1)): "The five parameters of the analytic function are profiled and their uncertainties from the fit to data are the dominant uncertainties." (Sec. 6). This is the dominant uncertainty on the limit per the paper.
- Jet energy scale: 2%, applied as a ±2% shift of the signal m_jj distribution: "We account for the uncertainty of 2% in the jet energy scale [86] by shifting the dijet mass of the signal distribution by ±2%." (Sec. 6). Stated as minor.
- Jet energy resolution: "varying the width of the signal distribution by ±10%" (Sec. 6). Stated as minor.
- ISR/FSR (µR, µF scale variation ×2): "changes the normalization by 10%." (Sec. 6). Stated as minor.
- Integrated luminosity: "The uncertainty in the integrated luminosity is 2.5% [88] and affects directly the signal normalization." (Sec. 6). Stated as affecting normalization directly.
- Negligible per paper: background function choice; pileup, PDFs, underlying event, parton-shower models: "The systematic uncertainties related to pileup, parton distribution functions, underlying events, and parton shower models are also found to be negligible." (Sec. 6).
- Acceptance uncertainty (Table 1 caption, paper): "The errors are dominated by the uncertainty related to the modeling of the jet radiation used in signal simulations." HEPData "Table 1" description instead says "dominated by the uncertainties in the parton shower modeling". Wording differs; both are recorded, not reconciled.
- Stat/syst split for the limits themselves is not tabulated. The ±1σ/±2σ bands are described only as "variation at the 1 and 2 standard deviation levels" (Fig. 2/3 captions).

## Caveats

- Mass coverage: limits only for 350–700 GeV ("Limits are presented for resonance masses between 350 and 700 GeV", Sec. 7). No limit inside 10 GeV–1 TeV outside that range; nothing outside the primary window was tabulated.
- Quantities are at detector level after the three-jet selection: σ×B×A includes the acceptance A from Table 1 (Sec. 7). Not directly comparable with a total σ×B limit.
- Narrow resonance only (width negligible vs ~10% resolution, Sec. 3).
- Data scouting with the 2016 HLT trigger (H_T > 250 GeV at HLT); the trigger efficiency is "larger than 99% for a dijet invariant mass greater than 290 GeV" [verifier w2_3, wR_a] (Sec. 3); the search is limited to 2016 data.
- The g'_q limits are for a vector coupling only to quarks, under the convention of Ref. [89]; the paper's text does not state which curves Eq. (2) is applied to; the tabulated g'_q values are used as given.
- Observed limits are the actual data; they are not projections or recasts. The CLs limits use the asymptotic approximation (Sec. 6), which is an approximation.
- Abstract claim "most stringent upper limits in the mass range between 350 and 450 GeV obtained with a flavor-inclusive dijet resonance search" is the paper's own statement, not verified here.
- Observed limits lie below expected for some points and above for others (e.g. 350 GeV obs 15.876 vs exp 23.125 pb; 400 GeV obs 11.903 vs exp 9.4688 pb). Interpretation (fluctuation significance) is not stated in the paper, so not reported.
